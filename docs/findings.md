## Overgrazing (2026-10-26; `docs/experiments/overgrazing.txt`, commit 6ac4da9)

8 seeds × 10,000 ticks, immigration off, DNA at its default. Arms: **A** switch off; **B** on (damage 0.02/bite, recovery 0.005/tick after 50 rested ticks); **C** matched random (the same damage per bite lands on random fertile cells, not where bites happen).

| | A off | B on | C matched |
|---|---|---|---|
| avgPop | 258 | 128 | 301 |
| leanLow | 172 | 81 | 210 |
| finalPop | 234 | 34 | 298 |
| runs extinct by 10k | 5/8 | 6/8 | 3/8 |
| births | 20145 | 7744 | 20487 |
| plants (avg) / edible biomass (avg) | 1145 / 1142 | 1419 / 1174 | 1246 / 1056 |
| grazed per tick | 9.0 | 3.3 | 9.0 |
| blockedMoveFrac | 0.21 | 0.14 | 0.36 |
| occupancyGini | 0.30 | 0.24 | 0.52 |

**Gate: B fails** (median per-seed avgPop ratio 0.61, need 0.8; leanLow 0.22, need 0.7; new extinction in seed 5). **C also fails** on one new extinction (seed 4) though its population and lean lows hold (1.17, 1.03). So `overgrazingEnabled` ships **off**. Read: grazing-driven damage cut the population to about half and its lean lows to a fifth, while the same total damage scattered at random did not (C is slightly above A). That points at where the grazers are, not the loss of food, as the cause of the crash. Caution: only 8 seeds, and the runs diverge chaotically (the early sweep's population ratios ranged 0.3–2.5 from small parameter changes), so B vs C is suggestive, not proven. The matched arm's total damage per tick is tied to the bites of that tick, so C also shrinks with the population.

- End-of-run `meanHealth` and `fractionDegraded` are useless here: in the runs that went extinct the ground recovers (B's 1.00 mean). Read health curves over time, not the end value. The experiment prints `A_off`'s health as 0.00 because it does not report it.
- Other diagnostics (first runs of `blockedMoveFrac`, `timeInPatch`, `departureFoodLevel`, `occupancyGini`, `scoutFraction`, taken over the last 20% of each run) are in the `.txt`; B's longer `timeInPatch` (33 vs 9) comes with a much smaller population, so it is not yet a clean behaviour signal.
- Perf (500-organism target; 400 founders with immigration, 1500 ticks, this container): 85–90 µs/organism-tick on vs 93–96 off, with different populations (282 vs 381). No measurable cost (the extra work is one 3,600-cell pass per tick); under 5% either way.

Parameter sweep (`npm run sweep:overgrazing`, brain, plants on, seeds 1–3, 2,000 ticks). Target: a crowded patch degrades within about 300 ticks. The tick at which the first cell fell below health 0.5 depends almost only on damage per bite (organisms eat about one unit a tick where they stand): damage 0.05 → 80–90 ticks; 0.02 → 180–220 in every seed; 0.015 → 220–430; 0.01 → 330–490. Recovery (0.005, 0.01) and rest (20, 50) barely moved it, and whole-region means stayed above 0.8 in nearly every run (peak share of degraded cells 0.0–2.1%), so degradation is a hot-spot effect. Chosen: **damage 0.02** (first degraded cell within ~200 ticks in every seed), **rest 50 and recovery 0.005** (a bare cell needs about 200 rested ticks to recover, the same scale as a season's lean stretch; rest 50 had the most seeds reach 2% degraded cells). The population ratios in the sweep (0.24–2.5 against off) are noise from chaotic divergence and were not used to choose. A softer setting (0.01) might pass the gate but degrades a crowded patch in 330–490 ticks, outside the target; not tried at 8 seeds.

Deviations: config keys are flat (`overgrazingEnabled`, ...), per `WORLD_RULES.md`, not `world.overgrazing = {...}`. Health is held per cell, not per patch, so it survives a plant dying and re-seeding. Windfall food does not damage cells. Plant upkeep is not scaled by health (only growth). No new sensor inputs or actions.

| DNA 1b.2 split diets (8 seeds × 3,000 ticks, 2026-10-26) | Plant eaters stay full plant eaters (herbivory 0.96) and do better: extinct seeds 2/8 vs 4/8, population +80, lean low +51. Kills double, but no meat-eating lineage appears in 3,000 ticks (carnivory 0.10). `docs/experiments/dna-1b2-split-diets.txt`. |
| Default world survival (DNA off, 8 seeds × 10,000 ticks, 2026-10-26) | **5 of 8 seeds go extinct**, 4 of them in an early lean season (seed 1 by tick ~800). Not caused by DNA (same with it off); probably since spontaneous generation went off by default. Worth fixing before relying on stage gates. |
| DNA Stage 1 gate (`dnaEnabled` off vs on, 2026-10-26) | **Identical.** Every metric (population, lean lows, births, kills, traits, abilities, brain drift, plants) matches exactly in all 8 seeds (Δ 0, 0/8 higher), as designed: the genome path draws from the PRNG in the legacy order. `docs/experiments/dna-stage1-gate.txt`. |
| Life-history genes, ship-on gate (2026-10-26; each gene on vs all six off, others off; `docs/experiments/lifehistory-*.txt`) | Gate = median avgPop ≥ 0.8, leanLow ≥ 0.7, no new extinctions. **Pass alone:** investment (avgPop 1.34×, leanLow 1.49×), torpor (1.26×, 1.11×), eggs (29.5×, 1.20×; the off arm collapses in several seeds, eggs lift mean population 258 → 574). **Fail alone on one-seed extinctions** (means are up): reproduction threshold (1.18×, 0.85×; seed 4), fat (1.31×, 1.44×; seed 4), base metabolism (1.18×, leanLow 0.00 median; seeds 4, 5). **All six together pass:** avgPop 635 vs 258, leanLow 490 vs 172, finalPop 706 vs 234, no new extinctions. Eggs are the likely rescue: a collapse that kills the other genes' lineages is survived as eggs. Shipped with all six on, because the combined world is what runs and it passes; the three solo failures are not gated apart, so run `--compare thresholdGeneEnabled=false,true` etc. with eggs on before concluding a gene is safe alone. Cost: `npm run perf` brain 65 µs per organism (earlier STATUS figure ~73; run overlapped another job, so rough, no regression seen). Not yet measured: whether the genes evolve (drift of threshold, investment, fat, torpor depth, egg rate, metabolism) over long runs. |
| Do tribes form from sharing? | **No.** Look-alike clusters come from family structure (children born adjacent with the parent's tag); clustering is the same with sharing off. |
| Energy sharing (×1.5) | Organisms exploited it by passing energy back and forth (32–39% returned within 5 ticks). Replaced by pure transfer, then by food sharing. |
| Pure-transfer or need-aware energy sharing | No effect on population or in-group bias. |
| Food sharing | Gifts almost entirely parent → own child. No in-group bias evolves. A weak hint (6/8 seeds) that it raises lean-season lows. |
| Does storing food pay? (evolvable carry capacity) | **No.** Lean seasons push capacity *down* (2.0 → 1.5). Upkeep outweighs storage while forage drive stays low. |
| Parents trapping children | Well-fed parents sat on food while starving children pushed against them. Two-way family pass-through fixed it, but produced sharper boom-bust cycles (peaks ~350, lean lows ~20–50). |
| **Dials vs brain** | **Brain populations survive lean seasons ~2× better** (lean low 79 vs 40, higher in 8/8 seeds). All 8 brain runs independently evolved "rest more, move less" (rest connection +0.5, move −0.4). Dials cannot express this: resting is fixed at 0. The hidden layer has not been recruited yet. |
| **Dials (with `restDrive`) vs brain**, 60×60 regions, before mobility | **The gap mostly closes.** Dials evolve resting too (`restDrive` +0.54; rest 10.5% of actions vs ~0.3% before), and the brain's lean-season edge falls from ~2× to ~4% (lean low 362 vs 348, still higher in 8/8; births +7%, 8/8). Most of the brain's earlier advantage was being able to rest. The hidden layer is still unused (strength ~0.3 for both, i.e. drift). |
| Crowding | 55–71% of all actions are moves blocked by other organisms; the 30×30 world is badly overcrowded. |
| Does a 60×60 world relieve crowding? | **No.** With twice the food, population averages ~400 and 65–70% of actions are still blocked moves. Organisms pile up at food regardless of world size; crowding is set by food per patch, not space. |
| Food in regions (seasons in step) vs food anywhere | **Lean lows double** (126 vs 62, higher in 8/8 seeds); average population unchanged. Organisms spend 86% of their time inside regions; the largest look-alike group roughly doubles (219 vs 113). |
| **Staggered region seasons** | **Lean lows rise ~2.5×** (333 vs 135, 8/8), and the population barely swings. More moves between regions (259 vs 203 per 1,000 ticks, 6/8): a modest sign of migration, not yet a clear one. |
| **Signals (dials and brain)** | **No meaning has evolved in 10,000 ticks.** About 4% (dials) / 2.5% (brain) of actions are signals, but organisms on food signal *less* than average (0.9% vs 3.9%), and moves with a signal in range head toward it 46–48% of the time (chance). The signal weights stay near zero. Signals cost a little: brain lean lows 369 vs 380 (lower in 8/8). Under the brain, signalling mostly replaces resting. Treat signals as an open experiment, not a result. |
| **Signals over 50,000 ticks** (`docs/experiments/signals-50k.md`) | **Still no communication.** Signallers now lean toward signalling on food (about 1.4× their usual rate, in 5/8 dials and 6/8 brain seeds, very variable), but receivers move toward signals at chance (49%) in every seed. Dial signal weights stay within neutral drift. Signals slightly lower the population (brain: 7/8 seeds). |
| **Mobility on vs off** (brain, 30×30, before regions; diagonals + squeeze + leap, all organisms) | **Trapping solved, at a cost.** Blocked moves 49% → 8%; average population 258 → 390 (8/8); lean low 77 → 100 (7/8). But resting rose 27% → 76%, births roughly halved, one look-alike group took over (largest 152 → 445), and carrying and gifts nearly vanished (carry capacity 1.43 → 0.29). Leaps were 0.06% of actions. |
| **Which movement ability wins?** (30×30, before regions; each founder gets one of diagonal, squeeze, leap or none, 25% each) | **Squeezing, in 8/8 seeds under both controllers** (89% of dials, 99% of brain organisms at 10,000 ticks). Diagonals stay at ~27% under dials but reach 82% under the brain (probably riding along with squeezer lineages; unchecked). Leaping falls to 4–7%, near what immigrants bring in; organisms with no ability die out. Not yet rerun on the 60×60 region world. |
| **Each ability on vs off on the merged world** (60×60, 4 staggered regions, brain, signals on; one switch off at a time, the others on; `docs/experiments/mobility-*.txt`) | **Squeezing is what matters.** Squeeze off → on: blocked moves 49.5% → 5.1%, average population 405 → 608 and lean low 340 → 524 (both 8/8); the ability spreads to 99% and resting rises 16% → 62%. Diagonals spread (69% vs 23% drift) but cost a little (avg pop 649 → 608, lower in 6/8). Leaping is selected against (8% vs 25% drift), used in 0.02% of actions, and costs a little too (627 → 608, lower in 7/8). |
| **Dispersal on vs off** (default world, brain, leap off; `docs/experiments/dispersal.txt`) | **A small benefit; crowding and clustering unchanged.** Children are born 1.75 cells away on average (1.03 with it off, 8/8). Blocked moves stay at 5.0% (squeezing already solved crowding) and look-alike neighbours are unchanged (0.85 vs 0.84), so family clustering is not set by birth distance. Lean low +12 (548 vs 535, 6/8), births +3% (6/8), moves between regions +21% (420 vs 346, 7/8). Evolved dispersal distance settles slightly below its neutral drift (2.1 vs 2.4): long throws are not favoured. |
| **Windfalls on vs off, signals on and off** (`docs/experiments/windfalls-signals-on.txt`, `-off.txt`) | **Windfalls don't give signals a job yet.** Organisms eat ~70% of windfall food whether or not signals exist (67% with, 70% without), so a call has little to add. No effect on population (avg pop −1 with signals, +8 without, 4/8 each); births +2–3%. Organisms standing on windfall food signal at 4.3% vs 2.0% overall, but receivers move toward signals at chance (47.6%) and the brain's signal connections stay within the drift seen with signals off (all within ±0.12). Still no communication. |
| **Carrion and predation, first look** (default world, brain; seeds 1–3 × 15,000 ticks, single runs, no control; run after the attack head start was eased, see Parameter log) | **Scavenging pays at once; hunting takes off in some worlds, in waves.** Every world scavenges from the start (thousands of carrion meals), and kills are common everywhere (2,400–31,000 per run), almost all of the killer's own colour (95%+): neighbours are usually family. Seeds 1 and 2 stay plant eaters (mean carnivory 0.03–0.08, no meat eaters, 490–640 alive). Seed 3 turned to meat around tick 4,500: the population fell to ~100–150 and half became meat eaters (carnivory 0.54 by tick 9,000), then plant eaters came back (315 alive, carnivory 0.10 by 12,000), and a second hunting wave began by 13,500 (190 alive, 56 meat eaters at 15,000). No world died out, and no hunting of other colours has evolved yet (kills of a clearly different colour stay at 1–8%). Watching seed 3 coloured by diet, the hunters held the two western regions while plant eaters held the north-east: diets split by region, which the species goal needs. An earlier version gave whole food units per body, so families bred and ate their young for free energy (one run reached 940 alive, all meat eaters, 57,000 kills by 12,000); fixed before these runs. |
| **Plants vs classic food** (60×60, 4 staggered regions, brain, defaults; `docs/experiments/plants-vs-classic.*`) | **Same population, faster turnover, a lower lean low.** Average population 637 vs 619 (higher in only 4/8), lean low 467 vs 540 (lower in 6/8), births +67% (27k vs 16k, 8/8). Organisms move between regions more (500 vs 374 per 1,000 ticks, 7/8). Movement abilities matter less: squeezers 58% vs 100%, organisms with none 26% vs 0% (8/8), presumably because food is spread over more cells. Gifts to children rise from ~1 to ~49 (8/8) and carry capacity 0.52 → 0.78 (6/8). Plants (~1,100 alive, ~650 edible units, 31 units grown and 13 eaten per tick) evolve from the founder means toward **faster growth (0.23 → 0.60), more roots (0.20 → 0.40), more lean dormancy (0.25 → 0.41), smaller seeds (1.25 → 0.72), earlier seeding and wider dispersal**, in 8/8 seeds. Lean seasons kill most plants (22k deaths vs 2.7k shaded and 110 grazed out): grazing rarely kills a plant. |
| **Mating, first controlled look** (2026-10-26; default world, brain; matched seeds 1–6 × 15,000 ticks, `sexEnabled` off vs on; `docs/experiments/sex.txt`/`.json`; run before DNA was merged and switched on, with the branch's phenotype mixing and the original one-child rule (each parent paying half a clone's share), before the life-history genes and the 2026-10-27 mating rule; none of those has been measured) | **Mating is used in every surviving world, but how much varies widely; no colour preference; parent–child pairs above chance; fewer organisms with sex on, cause unclear.** Seed 1 died out in the first few hundred ticks in *both* arms (new founders since the brain grew to 52 inputs; it was a stable world before), so it is left out below. Share of births from mating over the whole run: 0.1%, 1.0%, 7.6%, 11.1% and 24.4% (seeds 4, 6, 5, 2, 3). The `isMate` weight rose to +1.02 in the 24% world and fell to −1.50 in the 0.1% world, so selection goes either way. Look-alike pairs: 99.7% of matings against 97.6% of all ready adjacent pairs (chance), and `mateAlike` stayed near 0 (−0.19), so **no colour choosiness**. The test has little power while nearly all neighbours look alike. Parent–child matings: 13–16% in the worlds with many matings, against 5–8% of ready pairs (about 2–3× chance); `mateFamily` stayed near 0 (−0.17), so this is not an evolved preference. A likely, untested reason is that relatives carry the same willingness to mate, so they ask each other back more often. Population was lower with sex on in 4 of 5 seeds (mean 593 → 409 including seed 1), but seed 4 lost half its population with only 37 matings and its kills differed 4× between arms, so the arms' histories diverge and the drop can't be put down to mating itself. Unanswered asks used 6% of all decisions (per run on average, 709,000 asks for 3,563 children). Next: longer runs on the server, and the share of births from mating over time (History → Reproduction). |

## Engine

- About 16–18 µs per organism per tick. Default 60×60 world: dials 6.5 ms/tick, brain 7.5 ms/tick (peak ~490 organisms, ~10 ms). The 30×30 preset is about half that.
- Memory: about 5 MB for the world at 30×30, about 14 MB at 60×60. A persistent server needs about 512 MB RAM and 1 core.
- The simulation runs in a **Web Worker** (`src/sim/worker.ts` → `SimulationHost` in `src/sim/host.ts`), which owns the World and the tick timing. The page (`src/ui/simulation-client.ts`) only draws and handles input: each frame carries light organism records and food; metrics, history (only while Trends is open) and the selected organism's full record come at most 5× a second. Messages are defined in `src/sim/protocol.ts`. In desktop Chromium at 10× the page holds 60 fps with the worker at 60/60 ticks/s; if the worker can't keep up during a boom, ticks/s drops but drawing and touch stay smooth.
- The always-on server runs the same `SimulationHost` with any number of viewers (`server/app.ts`); the page talks to it with the same messages over SSE and POST (`src/ui/simulation-client.ts`).
