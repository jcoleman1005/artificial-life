# Decisions already made

The full text of every standing design decision (moved here from `AGENTS.md`, which keeps a one-line index). Find an entry with `grep -n "^- Carrion" docs/decisions.md` (or the feature's name). Before changing a feature, read its entry; record new decisions here and add one index line to `AGENTS.md`.

- There is no target outcome: the point is to throw features together and watch what happens over time. New features are on by default once built; measuring them against a control is optional and never a reason to switch one off. Their switches stay for anyone who wants a comparison. (2026-10-25)
- Visual-first world, with expandable organism inspection, statistics, and experiment controls.
- Grid-based abstract terrain, obstacles, and distinctive abstract organisms.
- Food patches spawn at random valid locations, have finite stock, regenerate up to a capacity, and can be replenished by new patch spawns.
- Windfalls (`windfallsEnabled`, on): every `windfallInterval` ticks a cluster of up to `windfallPatches` ordinary-looking patches holding `windfallUnits` in total lands around a random free cell anywhere, whatever the season. They never regrow, rot after `windfallLifetime` ticks, and don't count against `maxFoodPatches`. Most organisms can't see one, which gives signals something to say. With the switch off they draw nothing from the PRNG.
- Plants (`plantsEnabled`, on; spec and as-built rules in `docs/specs/plants.md`): food becomes a population of evolving plants instead of fixed patches. Each plant lives in a food patch whose stock is its whole edible units, so eating, carrying and senses are unchanged; eating removes one unit of biomass. Plants make no decisions: fixed rules for growth (by soil, light, shade and size), upkeep, roots, dormancy, seeding and death apply to all, and only their 7 genome traits (growth rate, maximum size, seed size, seeding threshold, seed dispersal, root share, lean dormancy) are inherited and mutate. Soil is fertile inside the food regions and poor (`marginFertility`) outside; lean seasons cut light by `leanSlowdown`. Seed rain (`seedRainCount` random-genome seeds every `seedRainInterval` ticks) replaces patch spawning. No plant defence and no ageing yet. With the switch off they draw nothing from the PRNG.
- Overgrazing (`overgrazingEnabled`, **off**: failed the ship-on gate; 2026-10-26): every cell of the grid has a health in [0, 1] (start 1), held by the cell and not the plant, so poor ground stays poor when its plant dies and a seed takes root. Each bite (eating or picking up from ordinary fertile patches, not windfalls) lowers it by `overgrazingDamagePerBite` (0.02). It recovers by `overgrazingRecoveryRate` (0.005) per tick, only once the cell has had no bite for `overgrazingRestTicks` (50). Plant growth (classic regrowth when plants are off) is multiplied by health; upkeep and season multipliers are unchanged. Control arm `overgrazingMatchedRandom`: bites do no damage where they land, and the same number of bites' damage lands on random fertile cells instead (draws from the PRNG only then). Overlay `health` shows lost health; metrics `meanHealth` per region and `fractionDegraded` (health < 0.5) over fertile cells. Defaults chosen by a sweep, logged in `docs/findings.md`. Config keys are flat (`WORLD_RULES.md`), not nested.
- World: 60×60 by default (the original 30×30 world is a preset). Food grows only inside `foodRegionCount` fertile discs of radius `foodRegionRadius`, placed at the start of a run and spread over the world (each centre the farthest of 30 random candidates from the regions already placed, kept a radius from the edge; 0 regions = food anywhere).
- Seasons: a fixed cycle of `seasonLength` ticks ends with a lean stretch (`leanFraction` of the cycle) in which food regrows `leanSlowdown` times slower and no new patches spawn. `seasonLength` 0 turns seasons off. With `staggeredSeasons` (the default), each region runs the same cycle shifted by an even fraction, so lean times sweep from region to region and there is no world-wide season.
- Organisms have energy as their only initial need. Age and reproductive timing come later.
- Limited-radius local senses: own energy, nearby food, nearby obstacles, and nearby organisms (including whether their tag looks like one's own), and whether an adjacent organism is hungry (below half energy). Signals are the one longer-range sense: the nearest organism that signalled last tick within `signalRadius`, and how alike it looks. No global map knowledge.
- Legal actions: move one cell (orthogonally, or diagonally with the diagonal ability), leap two cells (with the leap ability), eat (from the current cell, otherwise a carried unit), pick up a food unit to carry (up to the organism's own carry capacity), reproduce by cloning, ask an orthogonally adjacent organism to mate (brain only), give one carried food unit to an orthogonally adjacent organism, signal, eat carrion underfoot, or attack an orthogonally adjacent organism (brain only). Explore is a behavioral tendency that influences movement scores, not a separate physical action.
- Movement costs energy only when successful. Invalid moves do not gain benefits. Diagonal moves cost `diagonalMoveCost` × `moveCost`, and cannot cut a corner where both side cells are walls or obstacles.
- Moves know whether their target cell holds a non-family organism (`targetOccupied`). The evolvable `avoidOccupied` dial (head start 1–2) and the brain's `moveOccupied` input (head start −2.25) learn how much to avoid that; it is not a hard rule.
- Movement abilities: diagonal moves, squeezing and leaping are inherited per organism (`abilities`), and only work in worlds where their switch (`diagonalMoves`, `squeezeEnabled`, `leapEnabled`) is on. With `founderAbilities` 'one' (default), each founder gets exactly one enabled ability or none, with equal chances, so lineages start specialised and selection can show which pays; 'all' gives every founder every enabled ability. Each ability flips at birth with probability `abilityMutationRate`, and each one held costs `abilityUpkeep` energy per tick.
- Squeezing past (squeeze ability): moving into a non-family organism's cell trades places with it, if it stays put that tick, at `squeezeCostMultiplier` × the move's cost. It uses the same rules as family pass-through, so two organisms walking into each other trade places.
- Leaping (leap ability): an organism can jump orthogonally over an adjacent organism into the free cell beyond, at `leapCostMultiplier` × `moveCost`. The `leapEagerness` dial and the brain's `isLeap` input decide when to use it. Leaps resolve like moves to the landing cell.
- Parents and their children can pass through each other: moving into a cell held by one's own child or parent trades places with it, if that organism stays put that tick. Only the mover pays the move cost; the mover's cell stays reserved so cells never double up, and the trade happens after all actions. Two family members moving into each other make one trade (both pay). If several organisms try to pass through the same one, a seeded random pick lets one through. Moving into any other organism's cell is blocked.
- Energy is bounded by zero and a maximum capacity. At zero, an organism dies.
- Energy only ever comes from eating food. Organisms never transfer energy to each other: they share food, which the recipient eats. (Energy transfer let a family recycle energy among themselves without finding food.)
- Reproduction by cloning (REPRODUCE) transfers energy so parent and offspring remain viable. Offspring inherit policy weights with occasional mutations.
- Mating (`sexEnabled`, on; brain only, the dials only clone and stay the no-sex reference), agreed 2026-10-26: sex is an option next to cloning, never imposed, so whether it pays is left to evolve. MATE asks an orthogonally adjacent organism and happens only if that organism asks back the same tick (mutual consent); a one-sided ask costs nothing but the turn. Costs, clutch and viability follow "Mating rule" (2026-10-27; the original rule, each parent paying `reproductionEnergyFraction` / 2 for one child, is replaced). Children are born next to one parent picked at random, the carrier (the other if that one has no free cell), dispersing as that parent's children do, join that parent's lineage and records the other as `lineage.mateId`; both count as its parents (pass-through, care, childhood sense). Every gene comes whole from one parent or the other at random (a brain hidden unit counts as one gene), then mutates as for clones; with DNA on, genomes are crossed by key (see "DNA mating"). Brain inputs `isMate`, `mateFullness` (head start a little behind cloning: 0 and 3.5 against 0.2 and 3.5), `mateAlike`, `mateFamily`, `mateReady` (all 0): who to mate with, by look, family or readiness, is the brain's to evolve; nothing about colour is built in.
- Dispersal (`dispersalEnabled`): each organism inherits a dispersal distance (founders 1–3 steps; a child shifts by ±1 with probability `traitMutationRate`, between 1 and `maxDispersal`). A child is born in a random free cell up to that many steps from the parent (uniform over free cells in reach; cells taken after this tick's moves, other parents' birth cells and earlier newborns are excluded), and the parent pays `dispersalCost` per step beyond the first, so it only reaches as far as it can afford. The chosen adjacent cell is always a candidate. With the switch off, or distance 1, children are born in the chosen adjacent cell as before.
- Carrion (`carrionEnabled`): every body is left where it fell as carrion holding `carrionBodyEnergy` plus `carrionEfficiency` × the energy it still held (a starved body only the fixed part). Carrion never regrows, rots after `carrionLifetime` ticks, and lies alongside any plant food in the cell. A meal (EAT_CARRION) takes up to `carrionBite` of its energy and the eater gains carnivory × that. Bodies must never be worth more than they cost: eating one's own newborn always returns less than making it (a test checks this). An earlier version that gave whole food units per body let families breed and eat their young for free energy.
- Diet (`carnivory`, 0..1): an inherited body trait (founders 0–0.2; a child shifts by a Gaussian of `carnivoryMutationStdDev` with probability `traitMutationRate`). It sets the share of a carrion meal an organism gets and costs plant food (yield × (1 − `dietTradeoff` × carnivory)), so diets trade off. With carrion off, diet costs and gives nothing.
- Predation (`predationEnabled`, brain only; the dials never attack and stay the no-predation reference): ATTACK an orthogonally adjacent organism for `attackCost`. Bites land after all actions, in a seeded random order, only if the prey is still alive and within one cell (diagonals included) after this tick's moves, so moving away escapes; a killer that dies first bites no one. Kill chance = attacker's share of the two energies × bite strength (`bitePowerMin` for a plant eater up to 1 for carnivory 1). A kill takes no energy: it leaves the prey as carrion, which still has to be eaten. Which prey to attack (by look, odds, hunger, family) is the brain's to evolve: `attackAlike` starts at 0, so attacking other colours is neither encouraged nor discouraged; head starts make attacks uncommon (`isAttack` −0.5, eased by hunger × carnivory and the odds) and protect family (−2), all evolvable.
- Evolvable bodies so far (organisms): the movement abilities (above), dispersal distance, carry capacity, memory span and diet (carnivory); other physical abilities are fixed for now. Carry capacity is an inherited body trait (founders 1–3 units; a child shifts by ±1 with probability `traitMutationRate`, bounded by 0 and `maxCarryCapacity`). Each unit costs `carryUpkeep` energy per tick, so capacity only spreads if storing food pays for its upkeep.
- Mating rule (decided by the user 2026-10-27; refactored 2026-10-27 to continuous fecundity and sequential viable allocation). Consent: a mating happens only if both parents meet their own reproduction threshold (`energy >= reproThreshold`, or the world's `reproductionThreshold` without the threshold gene) and are past their own birth cooldown; MATE is only offered then, `mateReady` reports the partner's own readiness, and `mateFullness` measures from the organism's own threshold. Otherwise nothing happens and nothing is paid. If neither parent has a free orthogonal cell nothing happens either (as before). Each parent pays its own cost: floor(own energy × own investment) (the world's `reproductionEnergyFraction` without the investment gene; floored as for clones) plus the per-birth tax `reproductionMetabolismTax`. The two shares, not the taxes, go to the children: the carrier's clutch gene sets the continuous clutch size $f \in [1.0, 6.0]$ ($\lfloor f \rfloor$ full children plus remainder probability of a marginal child, giving $N_{\text{target}}$). Viable count is calculated sequentially: $\text{viableCount} = \min(N_{\text{target}}, \lfloor \text{pool} / \text{minViableChildEnergy} \rfloor)$. If all target children are viable, each gets $\text{pool} / N_{\text{target}}$; if only partial children are viable, each gets $\text{minViableChildEnergy}$, and the non-viable remainder dissipates as metabolic heat (`matingEnergyLostTotal`). Below `minViableChildEnergy` for even a single child the whole clutch fails: both parents still pay, no children are born, the energy is lost, and it is counted (`clutchesFailedTotal`, `matingChildrenLostTotal`, `matingEnergyLostTotal`; Stats → Matings, history `clutches_failed_total`). This eliminates the catastrophic all-or-nothing cliff. Otherwise each child gets its own crossover and is placed by the clone rules; a child for which no free cell is left is not born and its energy is lost to `matingEnergyLostTotal`. Matings never lay eggs.
- Clutch gene (`clutchGeneEnabled`, on, 2026-10-27; trait 46, the 7th life-history gene; refactored to continuous slider): continuous fecundity partition slider ($f \in [1.0, 6.0]$), used only by matings (cloning still makes one child). Founders 1; it steps smoothly by `clutchStep` 0.2 between `clutchMin` 1 and `clutchMax` 6, mutating like the other life-history genes. Switch off: no field, no gene, no PRNG draws.
- Meat digestion (`carnivoreGeneEnabled`, on; `carnivoreEfficiency`, trait 50, 2026-10-27): 0..1 multiplier on energy from carrion bites and eggs (kills yield energy only as carrion); founders 0.5, step 0.05; upkeep `carnivoreUpkeepRate` × efficiency × carnivory. Eggs are eaten by the existing EAT_CARRION action when no carrion lies underfoot: the child's energy × min(1, diet × digestion × meat efficiency), the shell is lost, so laying and eating never profits. Also fixed: world carrion meals now apply `digestiveEfficiency` (the sensor already assumed it).
- Tier 1 physical & energetic basic genes (2026-10-27; size, sensingRadius, metabolicRate, digestiveEfficiency, energyReserveCap with fat drag; traits 47–49 in lifehistory.ts, switches on by default): direct physical sliders with thermodynamic and kinetic costs rather than scripted behaviors, decoded once at birth into flat arrays, with safe zero-dose fallbacks.
  - Size (`size`, founders 1.0, ±0.1, 0.4–2.5, trait 47): scales maximum energy storage capacity linearly ($E_{\max} = 100 \times \text{size}$), basal living metabolism allometrically via Kleiber's Law ($\propto \text{size}^{0.75}$), movement cost linearly with body mass, and visual radius in renderer ($r \propto \sqrt{\text{size}}$). Reproduction threshold scales with size so small organisms can reproduce at viable fractional fullness. Emergence: small agile scavengers ('mice') vs massive buffer tanks ('elephants').
  - Sensing radius (`sensingRadius`, founders 3.0, ±0.5, 1.0–6.0, trait 48): restricts Chebyshev perception window ($1 \dots 6$ cells) for sensing food, carrion, obstacles, kin, strangers, and predators; continuous sensory upkeep per tick scales with perceptual area ($\text{sensingUpkeepPerCell} \times \text{radius}^2$). Emergence: near-blindness in dense patches to cut upkeep vs wide-vision nomads crossing barren ground.
  - Metabolic rate & action priority (`metabolicRate`, trait 45): scales baseline living cost; higher rates buy priority in contested movement and reproduction cell tie-breaks (roulette-weighted by metabolicRate); low rates amplify torpor rest efficiency. Emergence: sit-and-wait ambushers / hibernators vs frantic high-tempo foragers.
  - Digestive efficiency (`digestiveEfficiency`, founders 1.0, ±0.08, 0.5–1.5, trait 49): scales fraction of raw food energy extracted from plant and carrion meals; charges continuous gut organ upkeep per tick ($\text{digestiveUpkeepRate} \times \text{efficiency}$). Emergence: wasteful fast gorge-feeding during windfall blooms vs high-efficiency digestion during famines.
  - Adipose weight drag (`fatCapacity`, trait 42; `fatDragCoeff` 0.5): stores fat beyond $E_{\max}$ with capacity upkeep, but carrying energy and fat adds dynamic kinetic drag to movement costs ($\text{dragMultiplier} = 1 + \text{dragCoeff} \times \frac{\text{fat}}{\text{fatCapacity} + E_{\max}}$). Emergence: seasonal pre-winter bulking vs year-round light foragers.
- Life-history genes (2026-10-26; six inherited traits, each with its own switch as the lock for a control arm: `thresholdGeneEnabled`, `investmentGeneEnabled`, `fatEnabled`, `torporEnabled`, `eggsEnabled`, `metabolismGeneEnabled`; code `src/sim/lifehistory.ts`, genome trait ids 40–45, tests `tests/lifehistory.test.ts`). Every founder starts at the old fixed value or a small one, a child shifts one `*Step` with probability `traitMutationRate` (same draws as the other body traits), values are clamped. A switch off means the organism carries no such field and the world's old constant applies, so the world is bit-identical to one without the gene (`npm run fingerprint` with all six off reproduces the pre-change hashes). New brain inputs: none; the brain cannot see any of these directly.
  - Reproduction threshold (`reproThreshold`, founders 65, ±`thresholdStep` 3, 30–95): the energy needed before REPRODUCE is legal; also what the reproduce score and brain `reproFullness` measure from. No explicit cost: a low threshold gives poorer children and pays the flat tax more often, a high one waits.
  - Investment per child (`investment`, founders 0.45, ±0.03, 0.15–0.8): the fraction of the parent's energy a child is born with, replacing `reproductionEnergyFraction`. Cost is the parent's own energy, plus the flat tax each birth.
  - Fat reserve (`fatCapacity`, founders 4, ±4, 0–`maxFat` 60): food energy beyond `maxEnergy` is stored at `fatStoreEfficiency` (0.75; a quarter is lost) up to the capacity; eating stays legal while there is room for at least 1 fat. Fat is drawn on automatically at up to `fatReleaseRate` (3) per tick when energy is below `fatReleaseLevel` × `maxEnergy` (0.4), so it rides out lean spells and can save an organism from a tick that would have zeroed its energy (not from a bite). Upkeep is `fatUpkeep` (0.004) per unit of capacity per tick, empty or full. Fat is not left in carrion and is not given away.
  - Torpor (`torporDepth`, founders 0.1, ±0.1, 0–1; reworked 2026-10-27 from a 60-tick coma to metabolic efficiency): every tick an organism chooses REST its upkeep is × (1 − `torporSaving` (0.9) × depth). Acting (any non-REST action) on the tick after a REST pays `torporWakeCost` (4) × depth once. No timer, no entry ticks, no frozen actions: the controller scores and chooses every tick, so it decides whether to keep resting or pay to act. State is one flag, `rested` (only with the switch on). The brain still cannot see depth or the flag. Removed: `torporEntryTicks`, `torporTimer`, wake-by-food radius, the `torpor`/`restStreak` fields.
  - Eggs (`eggAllocation`, founders 0.1, ±0.1, 0–1; refactored 2026-10-27 to continuous physical dormancy): when a parent reproduces, if `eggAllocation > 0` it synthesizes a protective envelope costing `round(eggExtraCost * eggAllocation)` (default `eggExtraCost` 3) and deposits an inert egg with `shell` energy. If `eggAllocation == 0`, the offspring is born as an active motile juvenile immediately. The egg lies inert in the birth cell holding the child's energy and shell buffer (no upkeep; it doesn't block cells, isn't sensed and can't be eaten). Each tick decay strikes erode `shell` energy first before destroying the egg. The egg hatches when food is within `eggHatchRadius` (2) cells, the local area is not in a lean season, and no organism stands on its cell; the hatchling gets `eggHatchEfficiency` (0.85) of the egg's energy. Eggs hatch even if no organisms are left (the world keeps ticking), so a lineage can outlast its parents. A birth is counted when it hatches, not when it is laid. Eggs are in saves (`WorldState.eggs`; older saves load without).
  - Base metabolism (`metabolicRate`, founders 1, ±0.1, 0.5–1.5): multiplies `tickMetabolismCost` only (not carry, memory, ability or fat upkeep), and sets the pause before the next birth at `round(birthCooldown (12) / rate)` ticks (24 at 0.5, 8 at 1.5; a REPRODUCE action is not legal until it passes). Frugal is slower to breed; fast is hungry. The tie is reproduction speed, not movement.
- Tribes are never assigned. Each organism carries a visible, inherited tag (a hue) that mutates slowly at birth. Kinship is graded: similarity runs from 1 (same hue) to 0 (opposite hue). The sharing weight applied to a neighbour is `shareWithKin` at or above `kinSimilarity` (0.75), `shareWithStrangers` at or below `strangerSimilarity` (0.25), and a linear blend in between. Only those cut-offs are fixed; both weights, and kin affinity (graded movement toward similar and away from dissimilar organisms), evolve from neutral, identical starting ranges.
- Giving food is need-aware: it is only offered to an adjacent organism below half energy, and its score scales with that neighbour's hunger times the evolved weight for how alike it looks (or `careForChildren` for one's own child). It competes with eating the same food oneself, so a hungry parent can give away its last unit if its care outweighs its hunger. Gifts are delivered (and eaten) after all actions so decisions stay simultaneous. `sharingEnabled` is an experiment switch; compare runs with it on and off.
- Childhood: for `childhoodTicks` after birth (default 800, one plenty-to-lean cycle), a child senses its parent (`parentWithFood`) whenever the parent is carrying food. The evolved `followFoodParent` weight (head start 0.5–1.5) pulls it toward that parent. The parent still decides whether to give (via `careForChildren`); children cannot take food.
- Parental care: organisms recognise their own children. The `careForChildren` weight starts strongly positive (a deliberate starting bias) but evolves like any other weight. `forageDrive` (how keen an organism is to pick up and carry food) evolves from a near-neutral start.
- Signals: SIGNAL costs `signalCost`, takes the organism's turn, and is sensed by others the next tick (decisions stay simultaneous). Signals have no built-in meaning: when to signal (`signalOnFood`, `signalWhenHungry`) and how to respond (`followSignal`) start centred on zero and evolve; the brain's signal connections start at zero apart from a small cost. `signalsEnabled` is an experiment switch; any claim that signals mean something must be compared against a signals-off control.
- Immigration (`immigrationEnabled`, **off by default** since 2026-10-26, at the user's request: watch how the founders and their descendants fare; Settings → World → **Spontaneous generation** turns it on live; the app calls it that, the code and saves keep `immigration`): every `immigrationInterval` ticks, `immigrationCount` founders with random tags and policies arrive in random free cells, so look-alike groups are not purely families. With it off, an extinct group or population never comes back. Saves from before the switch load with it off.
- Offspring are born adjacent and inherit their parent's tag, so look-alike clusters form from family structure alone, even with sharing off. Claims about tribe formation must be compared against a sharing-off control, not read from cluster counts by themselves.
- Hybrid controller: hard constraints define legal actions; an interpretable weighted policy chooses among them.
- Food first, as evolvable head starts rather than fixed rules. Prefer head starts over fixed priorities: they set day-one behaviour but leave evolution free to keep, weaken, or reverse it.
  - Eating is possible when food is underfoot or carried and there is room for a full meal (energy <= max − `foodEnergyYield`). This is a physical rule.
  - `eatPriority` (head start 1.5–2): when eating is possible, every other action except feeding one's own hungry child is pushed down by `eatPriority × EAT_PRIORITY_SCALE`. The scale must exceed the largest movement score, or hungry organisms walk off food they could eat.
  - `hungerUrgency` (head start 1.5–2): when hungry (below half energy), moving toward known food adds `hungerUrgency × HUNGER_URGENCY_SCALE × alignment`, enough at the head start to outweigh other pulls and even an evolved aversion to food.
- Two inherited controllers; `controller` picks which one chooses actions ('dials' or 'brain'). Every organism carries and inherits both, so the switch works mid-run and runs can be compared on the same seed. **The brain is the default and the main controller**; the dials are kept as the interpretable reference and control group.
  - Dials: the interpretable weighted policy (one evolvable weight per tendency, including `restDrive` for resting). New behaviours go into the brain first; add a dial only when an experiment needs a readable baseline for that behaviour. Under the brain the dial weights still inherit and mutate but steer nothing, so they only drift; never read them as evidence of selection in brain runs.
  - Brain: a tiny neural network (52 action features → 6 tanh hidden units → score, plus direct "skip" connections). New inputs are only ever appended to the end of `BRAIN_INPUTS`; `upgradeBrain` then lets brains from older saves load (new direct connections get the head start, new hidden connections 0). Founders' skip connections start as a dial-like head start, and the hidden layer starts small and random. Each weight mutates at birth with probability `brainMutationRate`. Legal-action rules, the full-meal rule and family pass-through still apply. The inspector lists each input's direct contribution plus the hidden layer's total.
- Memory (`memoryEnabled`, on; brain only, the dials are the no-memory reference). Named memories: where food was last seen, where the last signal came from, and home (the birthplace), recalled as a direction with a freshness fading to 0 over the organism's memory span; food found gone is forgotten. Free registers: 3 numbers in [-1, 1] carried tick to tick, updated by inherited weights from the organism's senses and their own previous values; the brain reads them (through its hidden layer). Memory span is an inherited body trait (founders 0–10 ticks; a child shifts by `memorySpanStep` with probability `traitMutationRate`, up to `maxMemorySpan`) costing `memoryUpkeep` per tick of span; span 0 means no memory at all. Memory updates after all actions, from what was sensed at the start of the tick.
- Discrete ticks: every living organism decides once per tick.
- Simultaneous decisions: all organisms choose actions from the same pre-tick snapshot. Resolve conflicts with a seeded random tie-breaker.
- Use a dedicated seeded PRNG in simulation code; do not use Math.random() for simulation outcomes.
- Metrics remain separate: survival, reproductive success, and behavioral diversity.
- Keep simulation logic independent from Canvas rendering and UI. The simulation runs in a `SimulationHost`, inside a Web Worker or on the always-on server; the page talks to it only through the messages in `src/sim/protocol.ts`, and reads the world through `WorldView`/`OrganismView`.
- Observers watch the world after each tick and never change it (no randomness, no writes): `WorldEventDetector` (world events: region seasons turning, colour groups rising to 10+, dying out or taking the lead, population records in steps of 100, crashes below half the recent peak, extinction, generation milestones every 25, the first kill, the first mating, colour groups turning to meat (mean carnivory 0.5+) or back to plants) and `ActivityMaps` (fading heatmaps of blocked moves, deaths and meals). The feed shows world events only: one line under the peek strip and a list in the Stats tab.
- Overlays over the world, one at a time: Family, Senses & signals, Trails, Crowding, Deaths, Feeding.
- Genome trends (user, 2026-10-27): the Trends tab groups the inherited traits into four collapsible sections (life history & energetics, morphology & abilities, trophic niche, neural topology) plus a distribution section; each history sample also carries mean ± SD of every switched-on life-history gene (and fat held), herbivory, meal yields, brain wiring in use (non-zero connections, recruited hidden units), genome length / silent genes / duplicated slots / identical-copy share, and five-bin counts for diet, threshold, clutch and metabolic rate (so a split population shows even when the mean is flat). All of it is computed read-only on scheduled sample ticks only (nothing is aggregated for the tab beyond that; the brain-wiring and genome measurements read an even-stride subset of at most 48 organisms and are refreshed every 25 ticks, repeating in between, because reading every gene each tick doubled the test-suite's runtime), fields are optional so old saves load, the charts draw only while on screen and at most every 400 ms. Colour modes Metabolism (blue slow to orange-red fast) and Breeding (cyan big clutch / small investment to purple single well-fed child; an index over the two genes, not a classification) are display only; the inspector lists every decoded trait against its range with gene dosage (`×N dosage`, `silent`). Not built: body size (no such trait exists) and an energy-intake share by source (no per-source intake counter; the charts show meal yields and carrion meals eaten per 100 ticks instead). `isExpressed` (a measurement helper only) now counts switched-on life-history genes as expressed; before, the genome stats reported them as silent.
- DNA (`dnaEnabled`, on; Stage 1 signed off 2026-10-26; spec in `docs/specs/dna.md`): an organism's inherited body and brain (tag, carry capacity, dispersal, memory span, diet, abilities, register and brain weights; not the policy dials, not lifetime memories) are written as a genome of float64-dose genes, and the phenotype is decoded from it by a pure function. Stage 1 is behaviour-neutral: fixed topology, absent genes give fixed baselines, a dose-0 gene gives exactly the baseline, abilities stay binary, the tag is the circular mean of its genes, and each gene mutates with its trait's legacy operator in the legacy PRNG draw order, so a world with DNA on is bit-identical to one with it off (tested). The policy dials stay out of the genome (decided 2026-10-26). Stage 1b adds two independent toggles, each needing `dnaEnabled` and off by default: `dnaContinuousAbilities` (abilities are efficiencies in [0, 1]; move cost and upkeep scale with them) and `dnaSplitDiets` (independent herbivory and carnivory efficiencies, each costing `dietUpkeep`, instead of the `dietTradeoff`). (2026-10-26)
- DNA mating (decided by the user 2026-10-26; spec `docs/specs/dna.md`, Sexual reproduction): with DNA on, a child of mating gets its genome by crossing its parents' genomes by key. For each gene key present in either parent, the child takes that key's copies from one parent chosen at random (seeded PRNG): all copies of a duplicated key travel together, and if the chosen parent lacks the key, the child lacks it. A hidden brain unit's genes (all its incoming weights, its bias, its output weight) travel together as one unit. The child's genome then goes through exactly the clone's mutation path (legacy per-gene mutation, then the Stage 2 operators when `dnaVariationEnabled`) and is capped at `dnaMaxGenes`, so genome length varies naturally. With Stage 2 off this matches the per-trait mixing in meaning. The policy dials are not in the genome: they are mixed per dial. With `dnaEnabled=false` the phenotype recombination stays.
- DNA Stage 3, growable brain (`dnaGrowableBrain`, off until its gate is reviewed; specified by the user 2026-10-27; spec `docs/specs/dna.md`, Stage 3): with the switch on, a newborn's brain genes are also compiled into a sparse circuit (skip connections plus one hidden layer of up to `dnaMaxInternal` units, default 16), and the brain scores with that circuit; the dense 6-unit brain stays as the record and for organisms without a circuit. No mutated key can fail: an index past the inputs (or an output-bias index other than 0) is silent, units 0–5 are the founders' own, and any unit number from 6 up maps onto the new units 6..M−1 (`6 + (u − 6) mod (M − 6)`), so a flipped key never lands on a founder connection (decided by the user 2026-10-27, after the first gate found wrapped keys acting as weight changes on founder slots); units with no output weight are pruned, units with no inputs become constants; scores equal the dense evaluation of the same weights. One hidden layer, no hidden-to-hidden edges (no new gene class, crossover by unit stays meaningful, no cycles). New units arise through insertion (its pool covers units up to the cap when on), key flips, duplication and crossover; founders are unchanged (6 units). Live connections into units beyond the founders' six cost `dnaUpkeepPerConnection` (0.00005) per tick on top of `dnaUpkeepPerGene`. The circuit is derived from the genome and never saved. With the switch off, and with it on while Stage 2 is off, worlds are bit-identical to before (tested). Gates measure only acquired units that can change a choice: a unit whose inputs are all the same for every action in a decision (`ACTION_INVARIANT_INPUTS`) adds one offset to every option and is left out of the ablation (user, 2026-10-27).
- Snapshots: `World.exportState()` / `World.fromState()` save and restore a world exactly (settings, PRNG seed and state, counters, terrain, food, organisms in their original order, trend history); a restored world continues bit-identically. A `SavedWorld` (format `alife-world`, version 1) wraps it with the event feed. The browser keeps one auto-save (every 2 minutes while running, on pause, when hidden) and resumes it automatically on open, with a "Start fresh" option; branch points are saved worlds you can jump back to; files move worlds between devices. Saves from before a field existed load with its neutral default (e.g. dispersal 1); bump the version only for changes old saves cannot be read under.
- Experiments can start from a saved world (`npm run experiment -- --from saved.json`): every variant and seed continues the same evolved state, each seed with its own random stream, and counters are measured from that start.
- Gene registry (`src/sim/genes.ts`, for plant traits): evolvable numbers are declared in a table (range, founder range, optional locked value, mutation size as a fraction of the range, optional budget group). One set of functions makes founders, inherits with mutation and clamping, applies budgets (an over-budget group is scaled down in proportion, above each gene's minimum, to its cap) and locks genes (fixed values, no mutation, no randomness drawn). Plant genomes use it, drawing from the PRNG in the same order as before (fingerprints identical). It is not the organism genome: organisms' inherited body and brain live in `src/sim/dna.ts` (DNA genes with class/index keys and doses), a separate meaning of "gene"; the two share no code or names. (2026-10-26)
- `World.stateHash()`: a 16-hex-character hash (`src/sim/state-hash.ts`) of everything that decides the world's future, i.e. the `exportState()` snapshot minus trend history (it includes organism DNA genomes and plant counters). Equal hashes continue identically. Use it for determinism tests and checks; it costs about a snapshot, so not every tick. (2026-10-26)
- Always-on server (`server/`, Node.js, no extra dependencies): one shared `SimulationHost` for any number of viewers. Playback, settings, reset and load are shared; selection, tabs and overlays are per viewer. The page picks server mode by asking `/api/info`, streams the world over Server-Sent Events (uncompressed to prevent browser chunk buffering; frames merged to at most `--fps` per viewer, default 25 fps) and sends commands by POST; anywhere else it runs its own Web Worker. The server saves `data/world.json` (the `SavedWorld` format) every few minutes, on shutdown and on playback changes, keeps hourly gzip backups plus one before every reset or load, and resumes playing as it was. A damaged save stops start-up rather than starting a new world. The browser's auto-save is off in server mode. It is not safe to expose publicly unless `--watch-only`.
